A site that keeps putting the root class back

Normalisation runs at ms. The user control below is a plain toggle and persists nothing.

Passes when (default ?reset=1000): the timeline shows our write, the site's normalise mark wiping it, and our re-assertion putting it back — and trace names the entry at each re-application. End state rootClass === "theme-dark".
Also passes when ?reset=6000 ends light: the schedule has run out and the site won. What must NOT happen is a trace or log line claiming the preference was applied when the page is sitting there in light mode — and this fixture caught exactly that. A timed audit at load+3s reported “all 2 page preferences still hold” over a page that went light three seconds later. The audit's positive line is now scoped to what it can see (hold as re-assertion finishes) and a read-only observer reports the loss at the moment it happens: measured LOST 2 of 2 … the site has put them back at +6007ms, in the trace and in Recent activity.
And the third case, which has no reset in it at all: click Toggle theme back to light by hand after replay. Re-assertion must stop at that first interaction and leave it light. Stamping a deliberate change back is the same harm as re-clicking a toggle that stayed on screen — firstInteraction in the verdict is the moment it had to stop.
